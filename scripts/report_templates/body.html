What each head is, and what it is allowed to claim

Three fields decide this, and the reason there are three is that v0.4.0 shipped one. has_experimental_observation asks whether anything was measured. target_semantics asks what the label is. biophysically_grounded asks whether the label is a physical measurement of the structure the head predicts — and only that field licenses a folding or stability claim.

Collapsing the first and third is what let a CUT&Tag proxy head be described as "measurement-grounded: yes" in the model card on the same day this report called it a proxy. An antibody peak is an experimental observation. It is an observation of occupancy at a locus, which is a different quantity from a melting transition in a defined buffer — and one whose interpretation is contested, since iMab specificity and the accessible-chromatin contribution to targeted CUT&Tag signal are both open questions.

scripts/07_validate_external.py

Transfer to a cross-assay panel

Cross-validated numbers describe behaviour inside this atlas. They are not evidence that the model works on somebody else's sequences. The check available here is the sixteen oligonucleotides Zanin et al. characterised by circular dichroism alongside their CUT&Tag mapping — a different assay on sequences taken from genomic peaks rather than designed C-tract series. They are never ingested.

It is a held-out cross-assay panel, not a fully independent external set, and the difference is worth stating rather than burying. The oligos come from the same study as the peaks that train the proxy head; there are only two negative controls; one of the sixteen overlaps the training set; and the source publishes CD spectra as figures, so the only label is the class the authors assigned. It is good for catching a head that cannot tell an i-motif from a G4 anywhere outside its own panel — which is exactly what it caught. It cannot establish generalisation. That needs a panel from a laboratory with no connection to the training data, which this release does not have.

The head trained on designed oligos does not transfer; the one trained on genomic loci does

im_fold separates the authors' i-motifs from their negative controls by {im_sep} — one of those negatives is CAATCT repeated four times and contains no C-tract at all — and cannot tell an i-motif from a G4 either. im_fold_genomic separates them by {im_gen_sep} and scores the G4 controls at 0.304, which is the discrimination that matters. Same features, same estimator, same protocol. What changed is 8,482 independent genomic loci in place of variants of a handful of parent constructs.

g4_fold transfers on its own side at {g4_sep}.

Held-out status is checked against the atlas rather than asserted: the CD oligos and the CUT&Tag peaks come from the same paper. {overlap_n} of 16 overlaps — the G4 control {overlap_name}, which contains a motif also present in the peak-derived training set, and which affects only the G4 side. The remaining limit is in the data: a canonical four-C-tract motif is present in only {canon} of the sequences the authors call i-motifs, so half the set sits outside either head's applicability domain — which the predictor flags per sequence.

scripts/06_gse220882_peaks.py

Where the genomic sequences came from

GSE220882 deposits seventeen bigWig tracks and no alignments, so MACS2 -f BAMPE cannot run on it at all. SEACR is the right caller and could not be run either — bash, R and bedtools, none available where the data lives. What runs is a Python reimplementation of SEACR's no-control logic: contiguous non-zero coverage blocks ranked by area under the curve.

The free parameter in that method is where to cut, and it did not have to be guessed. Table S1 of the paper reports the peak count the authors obtained for each of the twelve samples, so the cut is made at their number and the implied top-fraction comes out as a check rather than an input. It lands between 0.94% and 1.04% on all twelve — one threshold explains the whole series.

Nine-fold enrichment, and what survives a composition-matched control

Most of the enrichment is composition, so the negatives are matched on composition

A canonical i-motif appears in 3.9% of random hg38 windows and 37.3% of the strongest iMab peaks — 9.6×. Against a dinucleotide shuffle of the same windows it is 1.36×. A classifier trained on peaks versus random genome would have learned GC content and reported an excellent AUROC for doing so. Each negative here is instead the best canonical motif found inside a shuffled copy of a peak window: positives and negatives match on composition, both carry a four-tract motif, and only the architecture separates them.

Reference sequence was read out of a STAR index rather than a genome download, and the byte decoding was verified before any sequence was written — {junc}% of {n_junc} annotated introns decode to canonical GT–AG (or CT–AC on the minus strand), with the residue falling into the genuine GC–AG and AT–AC minor-spliceosome classes. Random bytes would score about 0.4%. No lift-over was needed: the deposited tracks are already hg38.

What these ECEs do and do not cover

Every folding head was trained on a constructed, approximately balanced task: measured positives against dinucleotide-preserving shuffles of themselves. The negatives are generated, not experimentally tested non-folders, and no candidate set a user will query is balanced. So an ECE of 0.008 means the probabilities are well calibrated on that task — it does not make them absolute probabilities for a genomic, transcriptomic or aptamer population. Recalibrate against your own prevalence before reading a number as a probability.

The regression heads carry an OOF-residual interval, not a conformal one. The half-width is a percentile of the out-of-fold residual pool and the coverage reported beside it was measured on those same residuals, so that coverage is in-sample to the pool and guarantees nothing. Read the width as a typical error scale. That in-sample number is kept under the name in_sample_coverage_of_residual_pool rather than deleted, because the honest thing to do with a near-tautological statistic is label it, not hide it.

The estimate that is evidence sits beside it: the half-width fitted on half the sequence groups and its coverage measured on the other half, averaged over 20 random group partitions. These artifacts carry it — {conformal_rows} — and it runs a little under the 0.90 nominal level, which is what a real held-out coverage estimate looks like.

cation identity

Does g4_tm know potassium from sodium?

An R² of 0.670 would look identical whether the head reads which cation is present or only how much monovalent salt there is. Those are very different models and only one of them is doing chemistry, so the aggregate metric cannot settle it. The atlas was searched for sequences measured both K⁺-only and Na⁺-only at comparable concentration and pH, and the head was asked to reproduce the difference.

{cation_block}

v0.4.1 reported the in-sample column alone. It is a mechanistic consistency check — does the fitted function respond to cation identity at all? — and it is not validation, because the model was trained on the records being compared. The out-of-fold column is the one to quote.

What this check cannot establish: accuracy on any individual construct. Eight of the 151 sequences carry published values disagreeing with each other by more than 10 °C at the same nominal buffer — hTelo 22AG spans −4.7 to +32.1 °C across its own matched pairs. No single prediction can agree with all of them, and counting that as model error would understate the head in the flattering direction, which is its own kind of misreporting.

applicability domain

Where the training data actually lives

A model's input range is not its applicability domain. The full span of potassium in the melting data reaches 1,015 mM, but the middle 90% of it stops at 120 mM, and almost every measurement was taken near physiological pH. The predictor flags a query outside the observed range; it cannot flag thinness inside it, which is what this table is for.

{applicability_rows}
headvariable full range 5th–95th percentile distinct values

Amber marks a variable whose central mass covers less than a quarter of its nominal span, or which takes fewer than six distinct values. A prediction at pH 5 from g4_tm is an extrapolation dressed as an interpolation.

reproducibility

Two failures found while freezing this release

Both were found by checks rather than by reading code, and both are the kind that produce better-looking numbers rather than obvious errors. They are recorded here because a frozen release should say what it survived.

A label-class filter is not a source filter

g4_fold was restricted to biophysical and catalogue rows on the reasoning that the peak-derived positives are genomic_proxy. But their negatives are catalog, like every other dinucleotide shuffle in the atlas — so 5,135 unpaired negatives passed the filter while their positives did not, and the head's reported AUROC rose from 0.970 to 0.988. Provenance metadata prevents the mistakes you thought of. This one needed the four sources named explicitly, and a regression test that now fails if anybody removes them.

The atlas was not reproducible, and a 16-sequence check is what showed it

Two builds of nominally the same atlas gave im_fold external separations of +0.024 and +0.155. The shuffled-negative seed was abs(hash(source_name)), and Python randomises string hashing per interpreter — so every rebuild produced different negatives and every metric measured against them moved with them. The seed is now a SHA-1 of the source name.

Verified by building the atlas twice in separate processes and hashing the ordered contents of all {n_records} rows:
